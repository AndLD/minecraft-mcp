# Phase 14: Excavation and mining

_Estimate: 3.25 sessions._

**Depends on:** [13 Construction engine and building](construction-building.md), [7 Gathering, crafting, smelting](gathering-crafting-smelting.md) (optional), [9 Storage and containers](storage-containers.md) (optional).  
**Needed by:** [15 Structured gathering: pits and quarries instead of scattered holes](structured-gathering.md), [16 Site preparation: leveling, dug rooms, and opening doors](site-preparation.md), [19 Railways](railways.md), [22 World discovery, including caves and canyons](world-discovery.md) (optional), [24 Autonomy, the bot's own will](autonomy.md).

`collect_block` is deliberately conservative: it only digs blocks that touch open air, never tunnels, and never digs straight down (that is why "dig a 3×3×2 pit" is refused). Digging pits, quarries, tunnels, and mines needs its own tool, with its own rules and a plan you can preview.

- Branches: `feature/excavation` (the shapes, the hazard rule, light, ore veins, ladders), `feature/excavation-extras` (the rest of this list, marked *later* below), `feature/excavation-cave-sealing`, `feature/excavation-stair-sizing`, and `feature/excavation-staircase-target` (the last three found live, below). Built on the construction engine (`WorkPlan`, `PlanExecutor`, `SiteSafety`).
- **As built in `feature/excavation`:** shapes `pit`, `shaft`, `quarry` (default target 9 above the bedrock), `tunnel`, `branch_mine`, `staircase_down`, each a provider that validates its own numbers and echoes them in `preview_excavation`; `HazardRule` (lava or water in the block or any of the six that touch it ends that chain one block short; also an open cavity ahead (`cave_breakthrough`), sand or gravel overhead, spawners, chests, rails and furnaces, and unloaded blocks; results list every ended chain with the reason and the place); chains and dependencies (`branch`, `needs`: a cell is only dug when the one before it is open, so a branch never digs through rock on its own, and branches beyond a short main tunnel are reported `blocked_by_earlier`); torches by spacing with `no_torches` when they run out; ore veins mined by flood fill (up to 64 blocks, never into protected areas or next to fluids); **ladders** up the west wall of pits, shafts and quarries, placed layer by layer, so the bot climbs out by itself (the pathfinder climbs ladders; the e2e test walks out of a pit). Tools `excavate`, `preview_excavation`, `list_excavations`.
- **As built in `feature/excavation-extras`:** `exit: stairs` (a helical staircase around the footprint, walkable out of the pit; the water landing needs no wall stairs because the bot can also use them), `water_landing` for quarries (2×2×2 pond in the far corner, two bucket placements), `spoil_policy: discard_junk`, torch crafting on `no_torches`, and a spare pickaxe crafted when the needed tool is missing (durability awareness is this: no separate wear tracking). `seal_hazards` was dropped: the hazard rule never opens a face to fluid, so nothing needs sealing.
- **Known open item:** the stairs e2e (`test/e2e/excavation/extras`) failed intermittently on one side assertion (the block under stair step 1 being dirt), then passed 9 runs in a row; the cause is not found (see the e2e gotchas in [AGENTS.md](../../../AGENTS.md)). Revisit it at the final hardening gate with a repeated run of that file.
- **Cave sealing** (done, `feature/excavation-cave-sealing`, about 0.75 session). **Found live:** digging a quarry with `exit: stairs`, a cave that touched or crossed the footprint left unintended openings in the quarry's own walls and floor ("air walls" where solid rock should have stayed), and could leave a stair step with nothing solid to stand on; this affected the dug result and could stop the walk-out. Two related gaps, both fixed here:
  1. `pit` and `quarry` dig with `cavityCheck: false` (unlike `shaft`, which already checks): the reasoning was "open to the sky, no cave to break into," which only covers the open top, not a cave crossing the sides or the floor at depth. Fixed by giving them `cavityCheck: true` too, so every shape stops one cell short of a cave the same way (`cave_breakthrough`), instead of a quarry or pit digging straight through into one unnoticed.
  2. Even where the dig correctly stops short of a cave, nothing repairs a boundary, or a designed stair or ladder step, that a cave has **already** hollowed out before the dig gets there: the existing rule only ever avoids breaking further rock, it never places rock back. A **new seal pass** covers this: wherever a plan needs a cell to stay solid (a wall, floor, or ceiling cell at the boundary of `hazards: 'end_branch'`, and every ring cell of `exit: stairs` or the ladder column of `exit: ladder`) and that cell is already open and not the dig's own (the same `isOurOwn` test the hazard rule already uses, so mined cells and ore veins are never mistaken for a cave), a block is **placed** there (`seal_block`, default `cobblestone`, falling back to `dirt` when none is carried) instead of the opening being left as it is. This is what closes the holes the player found: a quarry wall or floor a cave had already opened, and a stair step with no material underfoot.
  3. **A sealed breach can be worked past, instead of ending the branch for good.** Where a column, tunnel, or branch meets a cave (`cave_breakthrough`), sealing the opening lets it carry on past the cave to its full planned extent (a quarry column still reaches its target depth, with the cave wall closed behind it), rather than stopping there permanently, which is the "affects the result" the player saw (a column or branch left too shallow because a cave happened to cross it).
  - Applies to every shape that excavates, not only quarry: pit, shaft, tunnel, branch_mine, staircase_down, and Phase 16's `room`.
  - Off with `seal_caves: false` (default true) for a player who wants to break into a found cave on purpose and explore it (`explore_caves`, Phase 22, is the tool for that); excavation defaults to closing what it finds, since a dig was not asked to open a cave.
  - The previously dropped `seal_hazards` idea (`feature/excavation-extras`) only ever covered fluids, which never needed it, since the hazard rule already stops one block short of lava and water and no face is ever exposed there; this is the missing dry-cave case, and it is the reason `seal_hazards` is revived here under the clearer name `seal_caves`.
  - **Tests:**
    - unit: `pit` and `quarry` stop one cell short of a synthetic cave the same way `shaft` already does; the seal pass places a block on a boundary cell, a ring cell, and a ladder cell that are already open and not the dig's own (mined cells and ore veins are not sealed); a sealed breach lets the column/branch continue to its planned extent; `seal_block` falls back to dirt when no cobblestone is carried; `seal_caves: false` leaves the opening as found; a genuine ore vein or the dig's own earlier cells are never sealed
    - integration: `excavate` on a quarry over a stateful fake world with a pre-dug cave crossing the footprint and the exit ring: the finished quarry has no open wall or floor cell, every ring step has solid material underfoot, and the column that met the cave still reaches its target depth
    - e2e: a cave carved through RCON crossing a 5×5 quarry with `exit: stairs` at one wall and one ring cell: after the dig, every wall, floor, and step cell that should be solid is solid (checked through RCON), and the bot walks out on the stairs without falling through
  - **Exit criteria:** a quarry (with or without stair walls) dug where a cave crosses its footprint or its exit ring comes out fully enclosed, with the walk-out intact, and a chain that met the cave still reaches as far as the plan asked.
- **Stair sizing** (done, `feature/excavation-stair-sizing`, about 0.5 session). **Found live:** the helical stairs are one cell wide and three cells tall, fixed; walking them (especially for a following player, not only the bot) is tight. Two changes:
  1. **The default height becomes 4** (from 3), for easier headroom; the default width stays 1, unchanged, so a plain "with stair walls" request looks the same as today.
  2. **Both are adjustable from the request that orders the quarry** (or pit or shaft): `stair_width` and `stair_height` on `exit: stairs`, so "a 5×5 quarry with stair walls, 3 wide and 4 high" or "...stair wall sizes: 3 width, 4 height" sets them. `stair_height` is `stairStep`'s cell count per step (currently hardcoded to 3, becomes the parameter, default 4). `stair_width` widens `ringAround`'s band **radially, outward from the footprint** (currently a hardcoded single-cell ring): a wider staircase is a thicker walkable corridor circling the pit, not a longer one, so the bot (and a player following it) has room to walk rather than balance on a single-block spiral; each layer's step becomes a `stair_width`-cell-deep, `stair_height`-cell-tall block of cells instead of one column.
  - **Stair width is never part of the quarry's own `width` and `length`.** Those two numbers stay exactly what the player asked for the dig itself; the stairs are extra cells beyond the footprint's edge, the same as today's single-cell ring, just wider. **Parsing note for the agent:** "a 5×5 quarry with 2×5 stair walls" is `width: 5, length: 5, stair_width: 2, stair_height: 5` — five numbers in total, not a quarry that is secretly 7 wide because the stair width was added in. `preview_excavation` shows the footprint and the stairs' own bounds separately, so a wrong reading is caught before anything is dug.
  - **Tests:**
    - unit: `ringAround` at `stair_width` 1 (identical to today), 2, and 3 (the ring band is the right cells, still clockwise, still outside the footprint by exactly that many cells); `stairStep` at `stair_height` 3 (the old default, for anyone who asks for it explicitly), 4 (the new default), and other values (the right cell count per step, still needing the step before it); the default height is 4 when `stair_height` is not given; `preview_excavation` reports the footprint and the stair bounds as two separate sizes
    - integration: `excavate` with `exit: stairs, stair_width: 3, stair_height: 4` on a stateful fake world produces a walkable band three cells deep at every level, not counted in the quarry's own reported dimensions
    - e2e: a small quarry dug with a wider, taller staircase (checked through RCON): every level's stair band is fully solid across its width and open across its height, and the bot (and a second real player bot) walks up it without getting stuck
  - **Exit criteria:** "dig a quarry with stair walls" gets a 1-wide, 4-tall staircase by default; "...stair walls, 3 wide and 4 high" (or "2×5 stair walls" alongside a stated quarry size) gets exactly that, with the quarry's own width and length unaffected.
- **Staircase target and width** (done, `feature/excavation-staircase-target`, about 0.5 session). **Found live:** `staircase_down` only takes a raw step count (`depth`), unlike `pit`/`shaft`/`quarry`, which take a target level (`to_y` or `above_bedrock`); judging how many steps reach a given depth is the player's own arithmetic today. It is also hardcoded to a 1-wide, 3-tall corridor, with no width option at all (unlike the exit stairs on the other shapes, which already gained adjustable width and height in stair sizing, above). Two changes:
  1. **A target level, the same as `quarry`:** `to_y` or `above_bedrock` (default `above_bedrock: 9`, matching the quarry default exactly), converted to a step count from `context.origin.y` the same way `QuarryProvider` converts its target to a layer count, capped by the shape's existing length limit (`too_deep` when the target is past it, the same wording pattern as a too-long tunnel). `depth` stays as an alternative for a player who wants an exact step count instead of a level.
  2. **`stair_width`**, digging that many cells across at every step (perpendicular to `direction`, unlike the quarry's `stair_width`, which widens a ring — this staircase has no ring, only a straight corridor, so each step becomes a `stair_width`-wide slice instead of one column) and **`stair_height`**, replacing the hardcoded 3. **Defaults for a standalone staircase are not the same as an exit staircase's:** `stair_width: 2`, `stair_height: 4` (an exit stair defaults to 1 wide, since it is a courtesy off to the side of a dig already done; a standalone staircase is the whole point of the dig, so it defaults wide enough for the bot and a following player side by side).
  - Direction (`north`/`east`/`south`/`west`) is unchanged: already a parameter, already covered by tests.
  - Cave sealing, the hazard rule, ore veins, and torches are unchanged: all already apply to `staircase_down` and need no new wiring, only geometry that is `stair_width` cells wide instead of one.
  - **Tests:**
    - unit: `to_y`/`above_bedrock` converted to the right step count (including `too_deep` past the length limit, and the "give either, not both" refinement `quarry` already has); a widened step digs the right `stair_width`-by-`stair_height` slice at each level, still needing the step before it; the default width and height (2 and 4) when neither is given; an explicit `depth` still works unchanged
    - integration: `excavate` with `shape: staircase_down, above_bedrock: 9, stair_width: 3` on a stateful fake world reaches the target level exactly, is fully walkable across its width, and a cave crossing one step still seals and lets the chain continue (reusing the cave-sealing integration setup)
    - e2e: a staircase dug with `above_bedrock: 9` reaches the target y (checked through RCON) with no extra or missing steps; a 3-wide staircase is fully solid underfoot and open overhead across its whole width, and the bot (and a second real player bot) walks down and back up it
  - **Exit criteria:** "dig a staircase down to 9 above the bedrock" (or a stated `to_y`) reaches exactly that level without the player counting steps; "...2 wide" (or a stated `stair_width`/`stair_height`) changes the corridor's size with no other behavior change; a plain request with no width or level stated behaves like a wide (2×4), 9-above-bedrock staircase by default, not the old single-cell corridor.
- **Differences and what moved to `feature/excavation-extras`:** the way out of a deep quarry is a ladder, not a spiral staircase in the walls: a 5×5 footprint has too few wall cells for a stair 119 layers deep, and the water landing only makes sense with a way back up, so both are *later*. Also *later*: `wall_style: stairs` (a helical staircase cut beside a wide quarry), `water_landing` with buckets, `spoil_policy` (the default already follows the storage policy), tool durability with crafting a spare, `seal_hazards`, and torch crafting (`no_torches` asks the agent for now). The cave-breakthrough rule is only applied to tunnels, branches, shafts and stairs at this point (a pit or quarry from the surface has open sky and no cave to break into, or so it seemed until a live test found a cave crossing a quarry at depth — see cave sealing, below); the staircase does not probe upwards for the same reason near its entrance.
- **Shapes** (each an `ExcavationProvider`; a new shape is a new class):
  - `pit` (width × length × depth, for example 3×3×2), `shaft`, and `staircase_down` (a diagonal descent in a stated direction, down to a step count or a target level, `to_y`/`above_bedrock`, defaulting to 9 above the bedrock; `stair_width`/`stair_height` default to 2×4 — see "Staircase target and width" above).
  - `quarry`: width × length (for example 5×5) down to a target level, given either as `to_y` or as `above_bedrock` (levels above the world's lowest layer: −64 in the overworld, so 9 means y = −55, confirmed; the Nether and the End bottom out at 0). **The default target is `above_bedrock: 9`** when the request names no other level. Dug top-down, layer by layer. `wall_style: stairs` leaves a **spiral staircase of solid steps along the walls** so the way out is always walkable; the **optional** `water_landing` (off by default, only when the request asks for it) digs a 2×2×2 pit at the bottom (kept away from the tunnel entrance) and fills it with water from buckets, so you can jump from the top and land unhurt, then walk back up the stairs. Buckets are fetched from the inventory or storage, or crafted from iron; without the option no buckets are needed.
  - `tunnel`: width × height × length in a direction (default 1×2). Starts anywhere, for example at the bottom of a quarry. **A tunnel has no branches unless they are asked for.**
  - `branch_mine`: a primary tunnel plus side branches on both sides, perpendicular, every `spacing` blocks (default 3: two blocks of wall between neighbouring branches), each `branch_length` long. Only used when the request describes branches or a mine; a plain "dig a tunnel" never adds them. The pattern reveals ore on both sides and above and below every branch.
  - **Sizes are numbers, phrasing is the agent's job.** Players describe sizes in many ways ("3×3×2", "1 wide, 5 high, 50 deep", "a 2-high tunnel, 40 long", "5 by 5"). The agent extracts width, height, length (or depth), and direction from the request and passes them as separate numbers; the server never parses free text. When the phrasing is ambiguous (for example which of three numbers is the height), the agent asks, and `preview_excavation` echoes the interpreted dimensions, so a wrong reading is seen before anything is dug.
- **The hazard rule** (the rule about lava and water, stated precisely). Before the bot breaks any block, it looks at that block and at the six blocks that touch it, and at the cells that will become the wall, floor, and ceiling beside the new opening. **If any of them is lava or water (source or flowing), the block is not broken and that branch ends there**: breaking it would let the fluid flow into the tunnel. With `seal_hazards` the exposed face is plugged with a solid block; either way the result records `hit_lava` or `hit_water` with the position, and the other branches carry on. The same check also ends a branch on: an open cavity or ravine ahead (a *cave breakthrough*, recorded so cave exploration can use it, and lit), a column of sand or gravel directly above the opening (it would fall in: supported with a block, or the branch ends `falling_blocks`), a monster spawner (reported, never broken), unloaded chunks, chests and rails and other player-placed blocks, and protected areas.
- **Safe vertical work:** the bot never digs the cell it stands on unless the plan is a designed stair, it never stands where the cell below is lava, void, or a drop of more than 3, and quarries are dug in layers from a ramp, so there is always a walkable way out.
- **Light** (shared `LightManager`): a torch is placed whenever the light at the bot is below `min_light` (default 7; monsters spawn only at light level 0 in 1.21, so anything above 0 stops spawning and 7 also keeps it visible), and at least every `torch_spacing` blocks in tunnels (default 6), on alternating sides of quarry stairs, and at every branch junction. Torches are crafted (coal or charcoal + sticks) or fetched; with none left the work stops with `no_torches`, and the agent asks.
- **Ores:** any ore that touches the dug space is mined, vein by vein (a flood fill with a size limit, under the same hazard rule). The bot does not use its knowledge of buried blocks to steer towards ores: it digs the pattern, as a player would.
- **Spoil:** a big quarry moves thousands of blocks. **By default it follows the standing storage policy** (`set_storage_policy`: ask, offload and return, or stop), like every other task; nothing new is decided silently. A request can say otherwise, per call or as a standing instruction: `spoil_policy: discard_junk` (drop cobblestone, dirt, gravel, deepslate, and the like beyond a small stock, away from the work) or `offload` (chests by the storage rules).
- **Tools:** the best pickaxe (and shovel for soft blocks); when durability runs low it takes the next one, crafts a spare if the materials are there, and otherwise stops with `no_tools`. Food and threats are handled by the reflexes, which pause and resume the work.
- **Progress and resume:** the plan cursor is saved, so an interrupted dig (a disconnect, a fight, a full inventory) resumes from where it stopped.
- **Tools (MCP):** `excavate` (a `shape` plus its parameters and the options above; sizes as separate numbers), `preview_excavation` (block count, tool wear, torches, buckets, and time estimate, and what is missing), and the results describe every branch that ended early.
- **Tests:**
  - unit: each provider's geometry (deterministic cells, dimensions, stair continuity, water pit placement, branch spacing on both sides), the hazard rule on synthetic grids (fluid at each of the six neighbours and at the wall, floor, and ceiling cells, cave breakthrough, falling blocks, spawner, protected area), `LightManager` (threshold and spacing with a fake light source), tool swapping and durability, ore vein flood fill with its limit, spoil policies
  - integration: `excavate` through the tool layer on a grid-based fake world: shape produced, branch ends on lava, inventory-full offload during a quarry, resume after an interruption
  - e2e: a 3×3×2 pit dug and verified through RCON; a small quarry (5×5, a few levels) with stair walls and a water pit: every step block exists, the bot walks out and in again, the pit holds water; a tunnel that stops one block before lava and before water placed by RCON, with the fluid untouched; a branch mine whose branch positions match the spacing; torches present where the rule says
- **Exit criteria:** "dig a 3×3×2 pit" works; "dig a 5×5 quarry down to 9 above the bedrock with stair walls and a water landing, then a 50-block tunnel with side branches every 3 blocks, torches all the way" runs to the end (or stops for a stated reason such as lava, no torches, or no tools) and reports what it found.
