# Phase 13: Construction engine and building

_Estimate: 2 sessions._

**Depends on:** [3 Movement and perception](movement-perception.md), [7 Gathering, crafting, smelting](gathering-crafting-smelting.md), [9 Storage and containers](storage-containers.md), [5 Persistent state](persistent-state.md) (optional).  
**Needed by:** [14 Excavation and mining](excavation-mining.md), [16 Site preparation: leveling, dug rooms, and opening doors](site-preparation.md), [18 Farming, crops, animals, and trees](farming.md), [19 Railways](railways.md), [20 Sleeping and respawn point](sleeping-respawn.md) (optional), [21 Signs](signs.md), [24 Autonomy, the bot's own will](autonomy.md).

**Design decision: one shared engine, not one big feature.** Building, excavation, and railways all do the same thing: turn a shape into an ordered list of block operations (break here, place there, light there), then carry it out safely, resumably, and within the bot's means (tools, materials, light, hazards). Their differences (what shape, what stops it) are small. Merging them into one feature would make one huge, untestable branch; keeping them apart would build the same executor three times. So this phase builds the engine and delivers **building** as its first client, and the excavation and railway phases are further clients that add only their shape generators and rules.

- **`WorkPlan`**: an ordered list of `WorkStep`s (`break` a cell, `place` a block at a cell against a support face, `light` a spot, `check` something), plus where the bot should stand for each. Shape generators implement `PlanProvider` (Open/Closed: a new shape is a new class): blueprints for building here, `ExcavationProvider`s in the excavation phase, a rail route provider in the railway phase.
- **`PlanExecutor`**: runs a plan step by step with walking between standing spots, tool choice and durability (swap to a fresh tool, craft one, or stop `no_tools`), material accounting (a **preview** reports what is missing before anything is done, so the agent can gather first), the inventory-full policy from the storage phase, protected areas, a persisted cursor so an interrupted plan resumes where it stopped, a verification pass, and retries of failed steps. Reflexes pause and resume it like any task.
- **`SiteSafety`** (shared by every client): the *hazard envelope* check (fluids, falling blocks, cave breakthroughs, drops: see the excavation phase) and the `LightManager` (reads the light level of the spot, places a torch when it is below `min_light` and no torch is closer than the spacing; crafts or fetches torches).
- `Placer` grows `placeAgainst(reference, face)`, so torches go on walls, stairs and rails face the right way, and blocks can be placed from the side or from below.
- **Building on top of it** (original scope of this phase): domain `Blueprint` (relative block placements) and the `BlueprintProvider` registry. Providers: `Box`/`Walls`/`Floor`, `Shelter` (1×2 emergency), `SmallHouse`, plus a JSON blueprint loader for custom builds. `BuildSkill` = `PlanExecutor` over a blueprint plan. Tools: `build`, `list_blueprints`, `place_block`, `preview_build`. Missing materials can be fetched from the container index (the storage phase's `FindItems` and `WithdrawItems`).

- Branches: `feature/construction-engine` (the engine, with a first building client) and `feature/building` (the blueprint library, the house, and pillaring up).
- **As built in `feature/building`:** the providers `house` (floor, walls, flat roof, a doorway with a real door, optional windows, torches), `shelter` (a sealed 1×2 room built around the bot from the inside; no origin needed) and `custom` (any shape the agent writes out as layers of characters plus a legend, in the tool call itself, so there is no file format to maintain), and pillaring up (`Scaffolder`). Differences from the plan text: (1) doors are placed with the bot standing in line with the doorway (`alignAxis` on a place step, honoured by `StandingSpotChooser`), because a door's orientation follows where the bot looks; (2) the default scaffold block is **dirt**, not cobblestone: cobblestone dropped by hand gives nothing, so the tower blocks would be lost (the tool description says so); (3) a tower left standing after a crash or a disconnect is **not** removed automatically (cancellation and failures always descend first); the executor does not persist tower records; (4) stairs, pitched roofs, and beds inside houses are left out: they need block facing beyond doors (stairs and roofs are in the backlog; beds come with the sleeping feature).
- **Pillaring up (scaffolding), in `feature/building`.** Reach is about 4 blocks, so a wall taller than that, a second floor, a roof of a tall house, or a tower cannot be built from the ground. When the `StandingSpotChooser` finds no spot within reach of a step, the executor asks a `Scaffolder` to raise the bot:
  - It jumps and places a cheap block under its own feet (dirt or cobblestone from the inventory, chosen by a `scaffold_block` parameter with a default), one block at a time, until the step is in reach; it does the reachable steps from up there, and when it is done, **takes the tower down again from the top** (breaking the block underfoot, one drop at a time) and picks the blocks back up, so no scaffold is left and the blocks are reused. The tower stands outside the volume being built when possible, or on a spot that a later step will fill anyway.
  - `preview_build` counts the scaffold blocks as extra materials (reusable, so it counts the tower height, not every step), and reports the maximum height it will climb (default 12, a parameter).
  - Safety rules: never pillar in or next to a protected area, under a ceiling with less than two blocks of room, over or next to lava or water, or beyond the height limit; if the bot is cancelled or the plan fails while it is up, it comes down first (the drop is never more than one block at a time). No fall-damage risk by design: the descent is by breaking the block underfoot, not by jumping off.
  - New pieces: a `Scaffolder` in the construction folder (uses `Locomotion` for the jump and `Placer.placeAgainst` for the block under the feet), a `raise`/`lower` step kind inside the executor, and tower bookkeeping so an interrupted build can remove a tower it left (the executor looks for its scaffold blocks when it resumes). Excavation (shafts, climbing out of pits) and cave exploration can reuse it later.
- **As built in `feature/construction-engine`:** `WorkPlan` (steps `place`, `break`, `light`), `PlanExecutor`, `StandingSpotChooser`, `SiteSafety` (lava in or beside the site; torch spacing), `Placer.placeAgainst`, the `LightReader` port, `BlueprintProvider` with `BlueprintRegistry`, the providers `floor`, `walls`, `box` (floor, walls, roof, a 1×2 doorway by default, optional torches), `solid` and `block`, `BuildSkill` (with `StorageMaterialFetcher`), and the tools `build`, `preview_build`, `list_blueprints`, `place_block`. Differences from the plan text: (1) there is **no persisted cursor**: the world itself is the cursor. Every step whose result is already in the world is skipped and a second look at the end verifies the result, so an interrupted build simply continues when it is run again (the task's resume descriptor holds the same arguments). (2) The order is **support-first**: the executor takes the first step that has something to be placed against, so walls grow from the ground up whatever order a provider lists them in. (3) The bot avoids standing inside the volume it is building when it has a choice, so the last blocks do not seal it in; the `box` doorway is the other half of that. (4) Tool durability swapping and crafting a replacement tool are **not built yet** (a block that needs a tool the bot lacks stops the plan with `no_suitable_tool`); they arrive with excavation, where tools wear out. (5) Pillaring up is **not built in this branch** but is a firm part of `feature/building` (below): a small building is reached from the ground and from the inside, and taller ones need the scaffold. (6) Missing materials are fetched from containers with the storage feature's withdraw skill, then whatever is still missing is reported.
- **Tests:**
  - unit: `PlanExecutor` against a grid-based fake world (ordering, standing spots, tool swap, material preview, resume from the cursor, verification, retries), `SiteSafety`, blueprint providers (deterministic output, dimensions, door position, material counts), `BuildSkill` (missing materials, scaffold counts in the preview), `Scaffolder` (climbing to the needed height, tower removal from the top, a ceiling or lava or protected area or the height limit stopping it, cancellation while up comes down first, resuming removes a leftover tower)
  - integration: `build` through the tool layer on a stateful fake world produces the expected block grid
  - e2e: build a small house on flat ground from supplied materials, then verify the block layout in the world through RCON; missing-materials report when the inventory is empty; a wall or roof higher than the reach is built by pillaring up, the tower is gone afterwards and its blocks are back in the inventory, and the bot stands on the ground
- **Exit criteria:** "build a small house here" gathers or reports missing materials, then produces a complete, enterable house with a door; a building taller than the bot can reach (for example walls 7 blocks high or a second floor) is finished by pillaring up, leaving no scaffold behind and the bot standing on the ground.
