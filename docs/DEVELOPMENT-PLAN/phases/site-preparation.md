# Phase 16: Site preparation: leveling, dug rooms, and opening doors

_Estimate: 2 sessions._

**Depends on:** [3 Movement and perception](movement-perception.md), [13 Construction engine and building](construction-building.md), [14 Excavation and mining](excavation-mining.md).  
**Needed by:** [17 Iron doors and switches](iron-doors-switches.md), [18 Farming, crops, animals, and trees](farming.md) (optional), [21 Signs](signs.md).

Three features that prepare a place for building or living, each on its own branch: `feature/site-leveling` (about 1 session), `feature/dug-rooms` (about 0.75), and `feature/door-opening` (about 0.25). The first two are clients of the excavation machinery and the construction engine; the third belongs to movement.

#### Site leveling (`feature/site-leveling`, done)

**Built as planned, with a few narrowings, noted here rather than left silent:**
- **Landmark saving (`label`) was not built.** `set_landmark`/`list_landmarks` are Phase 26 (discovery), not built yet, so there is nowhere to save a plot's name and bounds to. The result still reports `bounds` and `level` so the agent can pass them straight to `build`/`build_farm`.
- **The height survey looks 30 blocks above and 30 below the resolved level (`SURVEY_REACH`), not the whole world column.** A plot whose fill or cut goes deeper than that is not supported (an edge case for ordinary building or farming ground; a deep hole is `too_deep` at 6 by default long before that limit is reached anyway).
- **`MarkerFinder`'s search is centred on the bot, not the player** (the underlying `BlockScanner.findBlocks` has no "from position" of its own, the same as every other proximity search in this codebase). It also has to consider many more than a couple of nearest matches (`count: 400`, found while writing the e2e test): dirt and cobblestone are also ordinary terrain, so in a dirt-heavy area the closest 50 matches can all be buried ground with the two actual marker stacks never in the running. `radius` still defaults to 64 as planned.
- **Cutting and filling run as two separate `PlanExecutor` passes** (not visible from the outside, but worth recording): a single combined plan would refuse up front with `missing_materials` for fill blocks the cut has not produced yet, since the executor checks what is carried once, before anything runs. Cutting first, then building the fill plan from what the cut actually gained, is what makes "little or nothing needs fetching" true.
- **Tests:** unit for every pure piece (`facing.ts`: yaw snapping and the facing rectangle; `natural-terrain.ts`: the natural/vegetation/tree/player-made classification and fill-block picking; `height-survey.ts`; `marker-finder.ts`: a valid pair, one or none, more than two takes the nearest, isolation and height checks; `flatten-plan.ts`: cut top-down, cut vs cleared vs blocked, fill bottom-up, `surfaceBlock` only on top, `maxFillDepth`) plus `FlattenAreaSkill` itself (cut, fill, fill from what cutting produced, an explicit `fillBlock` override, `clearTrees`, `clearStructures`, every `level` mode including the `auto`/`y`/`markers` refusals and combinations, `too_large`, player not online/visible, fetching missing fill material from storage, a fill failure after the cut already finished); integration through the MCP tools on a stateful fake world (preview changes nothing, facing-derived and marker-derived plots, `blocked_cells` with and without `clear_structures`, `ambiguous_level`); e2e (`test/e2e/site-leveling`, real server via Docker) cutting a mound and filling a hole in a plot given by `origin`/`direction`/`width`/`length`, and refusing with `unsafe_site` when lava is in the plot.

Original plan:

Preparing a plot for building: "get me a clean field for building, 20x10, on the level I am standing on." Building, farming (a crop field, a pen), and railways all need flat, clear ground, and doing it by hand block by block is slow. This is the excavation machinery (break and place steps, the hazard rule, spoil handling) pointed at a *surface* instead of a hole: cut what is above the level, fill what is below it, clear what grows there.

- Branch: `feature/site-leveling`. Clients of the construction engine (`WorkPlan`, `PlanExecutor`, `SiteSafety`, `HazardRule`) and of the excavation supplies (`SupplyCrafter`, `SpoilDiscarder`, spoil policy); no new engine code is expected. Two small additions outside the engine: the player lookup (`EntityScanner`) also reports the player's facing (yaw), and `MarkerFinder` reads blocks through `BlockScanner`.
- **Tools:** `flatten_area` and `preview_flatten` (same parameters, the preview changes nothing). A shape provider in the excavation registry style, so it shows up in the same preview and result formats.
- **Parameters** (all numbers, like excavation: the agent extracts them from the request and the preview echoes what it understood):
  - **the area, three ways, in this order of priority:**
    1. **`width` and `length` given:** the rectangle starts **at the block the player stands on and extends the way the player is facing**: `length` blocks straight ahead and `width` blocks to the player's right, both read from the player's own position and view direction in the game (the yaw snapped to the nearest of the four directions, so the plot is always aligned with the world grid), not from the bot's position and no fixed compass direction. The player is the owner (`MC_OWNER`) or the player named in `for_player`. An explicit `origin` and `direction` still override this. The preview echoes the bounds, the direction it read, and the player's position it used, so a wrong reading shows before anything changes.
    2. **Corner markers (no size given):** the player marks the plot themselves by **placing two vertical stacks, each two blocks high, of dirt or cobblestone at opposite corners of the rectangle** (`marker_block` limits it to one of the two; both are accepted by default). The rectangle is the axis-aligned box between the two stacks, corners included. `MarkerFinder` looks for them near the player (default within 64 blocks) and takes only stacks that are **exactly two high, on solid ground, with air above and on all four sides at both heights**, so ordinary terrain (a dirt slope, a cobblestone wall) is not mistaken for a marker. Exactly two must be found: none or one fails with `markers_not_found` and says how to place them; more than two takes the pair nearest to the player and the result says which (or asks, when it cannot tell). Larger than 64 on a side fails with `too_large`. **The markers are part of the instruction, not obstacles:** they are removed with the rest of the plot and the blocks are picked up again (unlike other blocks a player placed, which are protected).
    3. Neither: the call fails and asks for a size or markers (the bot never invents an area).
  - `level`: **`here` by default, the level of the block the player stands on** ("on the Y level I currently stand"; the new surface is that block's height), or an explicit `y`, or `markers` (the level of the lower of the two marker bases), or `auto`, which only **suggests** a level in the preview (see below) and is never used without the agent asking the player.
  - `clear_height` (how far above the level is cleared, default 6, so a house fits; up to 24), `fill_block` (default: the cheapest block that the cut already produced, otherwise dirt), `surface_block` (optional, for the top layer, for example `stone` or `dirt`; grass cannot be placed by hand, so the natural top of an uncut area is left as it is), `margin` (extra cleared blocks around the rectangle, default 0), `clear_trees` (default true: logs and leaves above the level go, saplings and apples are picked up), `max_fill_depth` (default 6, how deep a hole may be filled), and `spoil_policy` and `on_inventory_full` as in excavation.
- **What `auto` level means.** The level is the height the plot ends at, and it decides how much work there is. `here` uses the height under the player's feet, which is the right answer when the ground is flat but a poor one on a slope or a hill: standing on a mound 4 blocks above the rest of a 20x10 plot would mean filling roughly 200 columns four blocks deep. `auto` surveys the height of every column in the rectangle and proposes the height that moves the fewest blocks in total (the median of the heights, which minimises the sum of the differences): most of the ground stays as it is, the mound is cut, the pit is filled. It is a **proposal, not an action**: `preview_flatten` shows the cost at the player's level next to the cost at the suggested one (blocks to cut, to fill, and to fetch), and the agent tells the player ("here would need about 800 blocks of fill, level 64 needs 40 cut and 20 fill; which do you want?") and then calls with an explicit `y`. Without the player asking for it, the level stays `here`.
- **What it does** (one plan, run by the executor, so it inherits resume, protection, and safety):
  - **Survey first.** The preview reads the surface height of every column: lowest, highest, median, how many columns need cutting and how many need filling, and the block counts: cut, fill, and the fill that the cut can supply.
  - **Cut and fill balance.** Cutting is done first, top layer down, and what it yields (dirt, stone, sand, gravel) is reused as fill, so the plot needs few or no materials from outside. Only the shortfall is fetched (`fetch_from_storage`) or reported as `missing_materials` with exact numbers, and a surplus follows the spoil policy.
  - **Clearing.** Everything natural above the level in the rectangle and the margin: grass, flowers, snow layers, dead bushes, vines, leaves, trunks (with `clear_trees`), and loose blocks. Order: top down, so nothing falls on the bot; sand and gravel overhead are broken from the side by the existing falling-blocks rule.
  - **Filling.** Support first, from the lowest cell up, each cell placed against a solid neighbour. Holes deeper than `max_fill_depth` are not filled (`too_deep`, with the place), so an open cave under the plot is never buried by accident. Shallow water is filled with blocks; flowing water that comes back is reported.
  - **Safety, all from what already exists.** Lava in or next to the rectangle refuses (`unsafe_site`); the hazard rule ends a chain at an unexpected cavity, spawner, or player-made block; protected areas are refused whole (`protected_area`); **blocks a player placed** (planks, fences, chests, doors, anything not natural terrain) inside the rectangle are never removed by default: they are reported as `blocked_cells` and the preview lists them, and only an explicit `clear_structures: true` from the player's request removes them. Nothing outside the rectangle and the margin is ever changed.
  - **Result** carries the bounds, the level, cut, fill, and cleared counts, what was gained or used, what was left (with reasons), and a hint: the `origin` and `level` are what `build` or `build_farm` should be given next. An optional `label` saves the plot as a landmark (`set_landmark`) so "build on the plot" resolves later.
- **Limits:** 1 to 64 blocks per side (4096 columns at most). A larger job is several calls.
- **Other features use it:** the crop field and the pen from farming and railway grading call the same plan builder for their ground; nothing here depends on them.
- **Tests:**
  - unit: the surface survey and the cut and fill counts over a fake height map (flat, hill, hole, slope, tree), area from the player's position and facing (each of the four directions, width to the right, an explicit origin overriding it), `MarkerFinder` (a valid pair, one or none, three and the nearest pair, stacks that are one or three high or attached to terrain are ignored, dirt and cobblestone, size limit, the markers removed and picked up), `level` resolution (`here`, `y`, `markers`, and `auto` only suggests, the suggested level being the median), the cut and fill balance and the shortfall, `max_fill_depth`, margin, `clear_trees` on and off, ordering (top down for cuts, bottom up for fills), refusals (lava, protected area, player-made blocks unless `clear_structures`), size limits, and preview equal to what the run does
  - integration: `flatten_area` through the tool layer on a stateful fake world: uneven ground ends flat at the level, cut soil is reused as fill, an interrupted run continues where it stopped, nothing outside the rectangle changes
  - e2e: uneven ground prepared over RCON (a hill, a hole, a tree, flowers, a shallow pond): `flatten_area` 20x10 at the bot's level leaves every top block at the level and air above it up to the clear height, and a strip outside the rectangle is untouched (checked through RCON); the owner bot faces each direction in turn and the rectangle follows; two dirt stacks placed at opposite corners define a plot with no size given, and they are gone afterwards; a player-placed wall inside is not removed; lava beside the area refuses; `preview_flatten` changes no block
- **Exit criteria:** "get me a clean field for building, 20x10, on the level I am standing on" leaves a flat, cleared 20x10 plot starting where the player stands and running the way they face, at that level; "level the plot between my two markers" does the same for the marked rectangle; neither touches anything outside the plot or anything else a player built, and the reply says the bounds and level to build on.

#### Dug rooms (`feature/dug-rooms`, done)

"Dig a room 5x5x4 behind this wall": the player and the bot walk to a hill, a mountain wall, or a cave wall, and the player looks at the wall and asks for a room. This is a new **shape in the excavation registry** (`room`, a new `ExcavationProvider`; no engine changes), used through `excavate` and `preview_excavation`. What is new is reading the wall the player looks at, the entrance, and the door.

- **Which wall ("this wall").** `WallTarget` reads what the **player is looking at**: the block under the crosshair and the **face** that was hit (the `LookTarget` port gains the hit face). The room is dug **into** the wall, along the direction opposite to the face's normal, so it goes straight into the rock the player is looking at. Only horizontal faces count: a look at the floor or the ceiling fails with `not_a_wall` and asks the player to look at a wall. The entrance column is the hit block's column; its floor is the walkable floor in front of the wall (found by scanning down the column in front of the face, with the player's own standing level as the fallback), so the entrance is at floor level and the player can walk in, whatever height they were looking at. Without a usable look (nothing under the crosshair within reach), or when the request names them, explicit `at` (the wall block) and `direction` are used; otherwise it asks. The player is the owner or `for_player`.
- **Parameters** (numbers, as everywhere in excavation; the preview echoes them): `width` (across, 2 to 24), `length` (deep, 2 to 24), `height` (2 to 8; for example 5x5x4), `entrance` (`dig`, the default; `player`; `none`), `entrance_length` (blocks of wall dug through before the room begins, default 1), `entrance_height` (default 2) and `entrance_width` (default 1, up to 3), `door` (an item name such as `oak_door`, or `true` for the best door carried or craftable; **one door per cell across the entrance**, so a 2-wide entrance gets two doors and a 3-wide one three), `entrance_at` (`center`, the default, `left`, or `right`: where the entrance sits along the room's front wall, as seen entering the room; `left` and `right` put it **against the left or right edge** of the wall, so its outer cell is the first or last cell of the front row, at any room width and with any entrance width), and the usual `torch_spacing`, `min_light`, `spoil_policy`, and `on_inventory_full`.
- **The entrance.** **By default the bot digs a 1 wide, 2 high entrance** from the face of the wall, `entrance_length` blocks deep and centred on the room's front (with an even room width and an odd entrance width there is no single middle cell: the entrance takes the left of the two middle cells, as seen entering the room; "the entrance on the left (or right) edge" is `entrance_at: left` (or `right`)); the room begins right behind it. The player's words change it: "I will dig you an entrance" is `entrance: player`, where the bot digs only the room and, before it starts, **asks and waits** (`waiting_for_input`, a `needs_decision` event) until the entrance cells are open, then goes on when the player answers `resume_task`; "the room only" or a hole already there is `entrance: none`, and the room starts at the first cell that is open; a bigger opening is `entrance_width` and `entrance_height`.
- **The door.** "Put a door here on finish" is `door`: at the end, when everything else is done, real doors are placed in the entrance, **one in each cell across its width** (so "a 2x2 entrance with doors" gets two doors, which the game joins into a double door, hinges on the outer sides; a 3-wide entrance gets three). They go in last, like the house door, turned to lie in the plane of the wall with `alignAxis`, on the entrance floor, in the outermost row of cells. A door is two blocks high, so with `entrance_height` above 2 the cells above the doors stay open and the preview warns about the gap; with a height of 2 (the default and the "2x2" case) they fit exactly. The number of doors is counted in the preview's `needs`. The door is fetched from the inventory or storage, or crafted from planks by the supplies crafter when the materials are there; otherwise the result says exactly what is missing and the room is still finished.
- **Light.** As in tunnels and quarries: `light` steps with `torch_spacing` (default 6, 0 for none) and `min_light` (default 7). In a room the torches go on the **floor along the walls** so the middle stays free; with none carried, torches are crafted (`no_torches` behaviour of the excavation extras); a lit room is checked against the light level, so a room that is already bright gets none.
- **How it is dug.** One plan of `break` steps: the entrance cells first, then the room **layer by layer from the entrance inwards**, each cell needing the one before it (nothing is dug through rock from a distance), the bot standing inside. The whole excavation safety set applies unchanged: `HazardRule` (lava or water next to a cell ends that chain, an open cavity that the dig did not make itself ends it as `cave_breakthrough`, the entrance direction being left out of the probes, sand and gravel overhead, spawners, chests and other player-made blocks), protected areas refused whole, ore touching the room mined as a vein (`veins`), spoil by the storage policy, resume by re-running. A wall of **player-built blocks** is refused (`player_block`), as always.
- **Tests:**
  - unit: `WallTarget` (each horizontal face gives the direction opposite to the normal, floor and ceiling rejected, the entrance floor found in front of the wall, explicit `at` and `direction`), `RoomProvider` (the entrance and room cells for odd and even widths (and `entrance_at` `left` and `right` against each edge, with 1, 2, and 3 wide entrances), entrance length, height, and width overrides, each `entrance` mode, the door step last, torch cells along the walls, limits, cell `needs` order, the probe list leaving out the entrance direction), the `entrance: player` wait and resume, the door fetch, craft, and shortfall paths (one door for a 1-wide entrance, two for 2 wide, the count in `needs`, the gap warning for an entrance taller than 2), and preview counts equal to what the run does
  - integration: `excavate` with `shape: room` through the tool layer on a stateful fake world with a stone wall and a fake look target: entrance, room, door, and torches come out as planned; a cave behind the wall ends the dig with the reason; an interrupted run continues where it stopped
  - e2e: a solid stone hill built over RCON, the owner bot made to look at its wall (`world.face`): a 5x5x4 room with a 1x2 entrance and an oak door (and a 2x2 entrance with two doors), torches placed in the dark, and every cell checked through RCON; `entrance: player` waits, then continues after the entrance is cut by hand; water or lava behind the wall stops the dig one block short
- **Exit criteria:** after "follow me" to a hill and "dig a room 5x5x4 behind this wall", the bot digs a 1x2 entrance into the wall the player is looking at and a lit 5x5x4 room behind it, and with "put a door here on finish" ends by hanging a door in the entrance; "I will dig you an entrance" makes it wait for the player instead of digging one.

#### Opening ordinary doors (`feature/door-opening`, done)

Today a closed door is a wall for the bot: the movement library's door option is off (its own comment says it "causes issues", probably on servers other than Paper), so `goto` into a house with a closed wooden door fails as unreachable or stuck, and the bot cannot leave a room whose door it (or the player) closed. This makes ordinary doors work; **iron doors and buttons are the next phase.**

- **Which blocks.** Wooden doors (`*_door` except iron), fence gates (`*_fence_gate`), and wooden trapdoors (`*_trapdoor` except iron), by a table in one place; anything else that is openable is left alone. An iron door or iron trapdoor is not handled here: navigation reports the reason `needs_switch` (the next phase deals with it) instead of trying.
- **How, and the risk.** First a spike with a Docker e2e: turn on the library's own door handling in the safe movement profile and see whether it is reliable. If it is not, the bot handles doors itself in `NavigationRunner`: when the route ends at a closed openable block, or the bot is stuck right beside one, it opens it (a right click on the block), walks through, and continues. Either way the result is the same to the tools: `goto`, `follow_player`, and every task that walks can go through doors.
- **Close behind.** After passing, the bot **closes the door or gate behind it** (default `close_doors: true`, also settable as a standing instruction), so animals do not get out of pens, monsters do not get into houses, and a house is not left open. A fence gate of a registered pen is always closed again. A door that was already open is left open, and one that cannot be closed (something in the doorway) is reported.
- **Never through a locked way.** A door that will not open after two tries ends the walk as `door_stuck` with the place; the bot does not break doors down.
- **Tests:**
  - unit: the openable-block table (wooden doors, gates, trapdoors yes; iron no), open state read from the block, the open-pass-close sequence, closing behind only what it opened, a stuck door, `needs_switch` for iron, `close_doors: false`
  - integration: `goto` into a room behind a closed door on a stateful fake world: the door is open while passing and closed afterwards
  - e2e: a small house with a closed oak door: `goto` inside and back out, the door is closed after each pass (checked through RCON); a fence-gate pen: the bot passes and the gate is closed again; an iron door built over RCON: `goto` reports `needs_switch`
- **Exit criteria:** "go inside the house" through a closed wooden door works and leaves the door closed; an iron door is reported, not attempted.
