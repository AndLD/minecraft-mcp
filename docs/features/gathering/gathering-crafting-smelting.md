# Gathering, crafting, smelting

**Depends on:** [Tasks](../core/tasks.md), [Persistent state](../core/persistent-state.md), [Storage](../storage/storage.md) (optional), [Excavation](../world/excavation.md) (optional).  
**Used by:** [Hunting and food](hunting-and-food.md), [Drop recovery](../storage/drop-recovery.md), [Construction and building](../world/construction-and-building.md), [Excavation](../world/excavation.md) (optional), [Crop farming](../farming/crop-farming.md), [Egg collection](../farming/egg-collection.md), [Tree farming](../farming/tree-farming.md), [Sapling collecting](../farming/sapling-collecting.md).

- `CollectBlocksSkill` (`collect_block`) breaks blocks nearest first and picks up the drops. The count is **blocks broken**, not items gained (stone drops cobblestone); the result lists what the inventory gained. Group names (`logs`, `ores`, `leaves`) live in `src/skills/gathering/block-aliases.ts`. A block is only dug when open air touches it (no tunnelling), it is not directly below the bot (no pits), no lava touches it, and it is not inside a protected area. It walks to within 3 blocks, retries once from 1.5 when the dig says out of reach, and gives up after 6 blocks in a row that cannot be reached. Expected stops are typed failures with the numbers in the message and in `detail` (`mined`, `requested`, `remaining`): `block_not_found`, `no_suitable_tool` (names the tools), `inventory_full` (call again for the remainder), `protected_area`, `unreachable`, `not_reachable_without_tunnelling`, `aborted`. A resumed task (after a reflex) only collects what is still missing.
- **Structured gathering** (`feature/structured-gathering`, Phase 15, found in a live test: a big request for a bulk terrain block was digging one nearest block at a time, leaving scattered single-block holes). `collect_block` gains `layout` (`auto` default, `scattered`, `structured`) and `structured_threshold` (default 5, lowered from an original 32 once the exit and site checks below made a small structured dig sensible instead of overkill). For a plain, one-for-one terrain block (`dirt`, `stone`, `cobblestone`, `sand`, `gravel`, `netherrack`; `src/skills/gathering/structured-gather.ts`, `isTerrainBlock`) requested at or above the threshold, `auto` (and a forced `structured`) digs a real `excavate` quarry sized to the count instead of scattering holes: `StructuredGatherSkill` probes how deep the matching block actually goes at the site (`structuredSizeFor`, capped at 4 layers, never wider than 5 by 5, square-biased), and skips the exit stairs entirely for a single-layer dig (a shallow depression the bot just steps out of, so a staircase around it would be pure overhead) but keeps them for anything deeper, reusing `excavate`'s hazard rule, cave sealing, and inventory-full handling unchanged. **The requested block must actually be at the dig site** (`structuredSizeFor` returns `undefined`, and the skill fails with `not_terrain_here`, when the very first cell probed is not a match) — without this check a request for a block that is not underfoot (mining a stone *wall* while standing on dirt, say) would silently quarry whatever is actually there instead, which only stayed hidden while the threshold was high enough that small, off-site requests rarely reached the structured path. When the site is not suitable (protected, unsafe, blocked, wrong material), it falls back to the ordinary scattered dig and reports why in `structuredFallbackReason`; `not_connected` and `aborted` are not treated as unsuitable and stop the request instead. The result adds `layout`, and for a structured dig `shape` and `origin`, alongside the usual `gained`. Wired into `CollectBlockTool` (`src/mcp/tools/collect-block-tool.ts`) as a thin client, not into `CollectBlocksSkill` itself, so the existing scattered path is unchanged when no structured dependency is wired in.
- **Open-vein gathering** (`feature/open-vein-gathering`, the default for ores): `collect_block` on ore names or `ores` (`search`, default `open`) gathers like a player. `OpenVeinGatherSkill` looks at the veins in view, keeps the ones that touch open space **and** have a walkable way to them (`CaveMap.reach` from the bot, `canStandBeside`: a vein in a sealed pocket is not open), and hands only those to `CollectBlocksSkill` (its new `accept` filter). When there is none, it runs the cave exploration (`explore_caves` with `stopOnFind: true`, `returnOut: false`: lit, hazard-aware, ends the moment a wanted vein with a way to it is in view, and stays there) and digs what it found; this repeats up to 12 rounds within `max_minutes` (default 10). It never tunnels in `open`: with no open vein it fails `no_open_vein`, and the message names the nearest buried vein and the search to use. `search: open_then_tunnel` then digs a route to that buried vein with `excavate` `dig_to` (x-ray, on request, one vein at a time; a route that leaves the vein standing ends the loop); `search: nearest` is the earlier behaviour (nearest vein in the loaded area that touches open space, no search). Anything that is not an ore (logs, dirt, stone, mixed lists) keeps the earlier behaviour whatever `search` says. Result: `layout: open_veins`, `explored`, `tunnelled`. Tests: `test/integration/open-vein-flow.test.ts` (in view, cave search, several veins, sealed pocket, buried vein, no tool, full inventory, time, abort, lava-side veins) and `test/e2e/open-veins/` (a vein 60 blocks down a tunnel is walked to and dug, a buried vein is refused and then tunnelled to on request).
- `Digger.assess` decides tool use from the block data (`harvestTools`, `material`); `chooseTool` (`src/bot/tool-choice.ts`) is pure. A tool is only required when the block has `harvestTools`.
- `DropCollector` walks onto dropped items (each item tried once, at most 12 trips) so the game picks them up; `pickup_items` uses it too.
- `RecipePlanner` (pure, over `RecipeBook`) turns "make X" into ordered craft steps with automatic sub-crafts. Minecraft data has one recipe per wood type, so it only tries variants whose ingredients can be had (fixpoint over what is carried), best first, and backtracks on quantities; it merges neighbouring identical crafts and, when the materials fall short, reports what is missing for the oak variant. `craft` always makes `count` **more** (ignores stock of the goal item). `CraftItemSkill` places or walks to a crafting table when a step needs one, and crafts a table first when there is none anywhere. Each step is verified against the inventory and retried (3 tries) when the game ignored it.
- `SmeltItemsSkill` (`smelt`): smeltable items are our own table (`smelting-table.ts`; Minecraft data has no smelting recipes). Fuel is chosen by `chooseFuel` (charcoal, coal, sticks, planks, logs; never coal blocks, blaze rods, or lava buckets unless named; never the items being smelted). It crafts a furnace when there is none, works in batches of one stack, and times out after 10 s per item plus 30 s.
  - **The wait does not stand over the furnace.** After loading, the bot closes the furnace window and waits with it closed; it looks into the furnace again after about half of what is left (`min(60 s, max(5 s, remaining / 2))`), takes the output, loads the next stack, and closes it again. A 64-stack costs a handful of reopenings, not one look a second, and a chat message or a new task can come in while it waits (`cancel_task`, or `replace: true`).
  - **A cut-short wait leaves the furnace burning.** Cancelling (or a reflex, or `stop`) does not take the items out of a loaded furnace: `SmeltJobs` (`src/state/smelt-jobs.ts`, per world, `ProfileScoped`) records the furnace, item, count, how much was loaded and collected, and the fuel. The result says how many are collected and where the rest is. The next `smelt` of the same item goes back to the job (walks to the furnace, takes what is done, carries on), and `smelt` with `collect: true` (item optional) takes what is done and leaves the rest cooking. The session briefing lists `smeltJobs`. `wait: 'start_only'` loads the furnace and returns at once, leaving a job the same way. A job that ends by itself (done, timeout, out of fuel, a server error) takes the leftovers back as before and forgets the job; a furnace that cannot be opened any more drops its job. The result has `pending` (items still in the furnace) and `furnace`.
  - **Blast furnace and smoker** (`stations.ts`; `station: auto | furnace | blast_furnace | smoker`, default `auto`). A blast furnace smelts ores and raw metals (raw iron, gold, and copper, their ores, ancient debris) and a smoker cooks food and dries kelp, each in **5 seconds per item instead of 10, burning fuel at the same rate**, so one coal smelts 16 items there. With `auto` the bot uses a blast furnace or smoker that stands within 32 blocks or is carried when it can smelt the item, and a furnace otherwise (crafted when there is none, as before): cooking after `hunt_animals` or `fish`, and smelting ore, get faster by themselves once the player has built the block. Naming a station that cannot smelt the item fails `wrong_station`; naming a blast furnace or smoker that is not there fails `no_blast_furnace` or `no_smoker` and says what it is made of: they are **not crafted on the way** (a blast furnace costs 5 iron ingots). The station's time is used for the wait (poll interval and timeout) and for the `smelt_ready` estimate (the job records its `station`), and its speed for the fuel (`chooseFuel`, `totalFuelCapacity`, the top-up, and fuel from storage take a `speed`). The result has `station`. The adapter (`MineflayerFurnaceAccess`) opens all three windows; it used to accept only names ending in `furnace`, which left the smoker out. Not built: crafting either, and nuggets from tools and armor; the campfire is planned as [Phase 43](../../DEVELOPMENT-PLAN/phases/campfire-cooking.md).
  - **Out of fuel** is judged by time, not by the flame: the furnace reads as not burning with an empty fuel slot right after a fuel item starts burning, and again after the window is reopened (found by the e2e run, where a 3-item job gave up after 2 ingots). A furnace with an empty fuel slot whose input has not changed for 30 seconds has stopped: `out_of_fuel`.
  - **Fuel is topped up at the looks.** How many items a job smelts is limited by **all** the usable fuel in the bag (`totalFuelCapacity`), not by the first kind: the job starts with the fuel that covers the most (or the one named), and at each look, when the fuel slot is empty and items are still to cook, the bot puts in more from the bag: the kind the job started with while it has some, otherwise by the usual order (never the items still to be smelted). An empty slot proves nothing about the flame, but a spare item put in is not lost: what is unused comes back out when the job ends. So `smelt 200 raw_iron` with coal and planks in the bag runs in stages. The result's `fuelUsed` says what was really burned (put in, less what came back), per kind. A job that runs out of the bag's fuel ends `out_of_fuel` with the rest in the furnace.
  - **The `smelt_ready` signal** (`SmeltJobWatcher`, `smelt-job-watcher.ts`, wired in `createApp()`): a job left in a furnace (`start_only`, or a wait that was cut short: the record's `detached` flag) is announced through the agent's inbox (`wait_for_chat` events) when its time is up (`startedAt` plus 10 seconds per item), so the bot can do other work meanwhile and the agent then calls `smelt` with `collect: true`. It uses only the clock: it does not open the furnace or walk anywhere. It is sent once per job (`readyNotified` is saved), a job that a `smelt` task is waiting for is not announced (that task ends by itself), the timers are rebuilt whenever the jobs change or a world opens, and a job that finished while the bot was away is announced at once after a restart. The message says when the furnace is more than 64 blocks away or in another dimension; the job record keeps its `dimension`, and collecting from another dimension fails `furnace_far` without walking (the job is kept). A collect that leaves items after the estimate has passed does not ask for the signal again. The session briefing's `smeltJobs` also lists each job's `dimension` and `readyInSeconds`. **Fuel from storage** (`fuel_from_storage: true`, off by default): before the furnace is used, what the bag's fuel does not cover is taken out of registered, unprotected containers (`FuelSupply`, `fuel-supply.ts`, through `withdraw_items`: nearest container first, protected ones never opened): charcoal, then coal, or only the fuel that is named; coal blocks, blaze rods, and lava buckets are never taken by themselves. It takes only as much as the missing items need (one coal smelts eight). A chest that was registered but never opened has no remembered contents, so when memory has none the bot looks into the known unprotected containers once and tries again. The result's `fuelFetched` says what came out of storage; with no fuel anywhere the job still fails `no_fuel`. It is done at the start, not mid-job: a job that runs out of fuel later ends `out_of_fuel` with the rest in the furnace.
- **Gotcha found in a live session: a single starved reading of the furnace right after loading is not real "out of fuel".** A fuel item is consumed into the burn queue the instant it starts burning (the fuel slot empties at once), a moment before the server's separate burning-progress packet catches up (the same kind of stale-window read crafting and chests have); a single-item batch hits this every time, since the fuel and input finish loading almost together. Reading `burning: false` and no fuel item in that gap and believing it at once loses a log that had, in fact, just started burning (once consumed, it can no longer be taken back). The fix: the "out of fuel" reading must survive one full poll (`POLL_MS`, 1 s) before it is believed.
- **Mineflayer world lookups need `Vec3` positions.** `bot.blockAt({ x, y, z })` crashes with "pos.floored is not a function". Every adapter converts with `toVec3` (`src/bot/vec3.ts`); `FakeBot.blockAt` throws the same error for plain positions so a missed conversion fails a unit test.
- **Mineflayer's `bot.craft` is unreliable on 1.21** (found by the e2e tests, not fixable by retries): it predicts clicks on the client, and since 1.17 the server silently ignores a click whose window state is stale, so crafts fail half way (ingredients left in the grid, a stack left on the cursor, "missing ingredient"). `WindowCrafter` (`src/bot/window-crafter.ts`) crafts by clicking instead: after every click it waits a few ticks, after every grid change it syncs the window with the server, it checks that the server offered the expected result, and takes it with a shift-click. It is installed as `bot.craft` by `installWindowCrafter` **after spawn** (Mineflayer injects its own craft plugin as the bot joins and would overwrite it earlier).
- Protected areas (`protect_area`, `unprotect_area`, `list_protected_areas`) are boxes saved per world by `ProtectedAreas`; `collect_block` consults them through `AreaGuard`.
