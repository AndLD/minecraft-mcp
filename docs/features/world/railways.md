# Railways

**Depends on:** [Construction and building](construction-and-building.md), [Excavation](excavation.md), [Storage](../storage/storage.md) (optional).  
**Used by:** no other feature directly.

Phase 19 (`feature/railways`). A railway is a route plus a `WorkPlan` carried out by the shared `PlanExecutor` (cutting, bridging, lighting, and placing are the engine's own), with the hazard rule on. New here: where the line runs and how high (`RailRoutePlanner`), what is powered and how (`assignPower`, `buildRailwayPlan`), checking and repairing the result (`RailVerifier`), and putting a cart on it.

- Branch: `feature/railways`. New ports `RailAccess` (`src/core/rail.port.ts`: a rail block's `name`, `shape`, and `powered` from its blockstate) and `MinecartPlacer` (`src/core/minecart.port.ts`: right-click a rail with a minecart from the inventory), implemented by `MineflayerRailAccess` and `MineflayerMinecarts` (which waits for the cart entity to appear, so a click that did nothing is a failure). `RailwayLog` (`src/state/railway-log.ts`, per world) remembers the lines built (start, end, layout, variant, rails, label) so a cart can go "at the start of the railway" without coordinates.
- **The route, in two searches** (`src/skills/railway/rail-route-planner.ts`). First the horizontal path (`path-search.ts`, A* over ground column *and* heading so a turn can cost something): straight runs are cheap, a turn costs 4, a rise costs by its size, water columns cost extra; lava, unloaded terrain, **player-made blocks** (anything that is not natural terrain: `TerrainReader`, `structure`), and protected areas cannot be entered (only the two end columns are exempt). Then how high each rail sits (a dynamic programme over levels, one block up or down per rail at most, **corners and the rails either side of them level**): for each column and level it prices cutting what is in the rail cell and the headroom (natural blocks, plants, trees only; anything else, water, lava, or an existing rail makes the level impossible), laying a support block where the ground is missing (a *deck*: how a dip or a lake is bridged, placed against the neighbouring deck block), and sand or gravel overhead. When no level works at some column, that column is blocked and the horizontal search runs again (up to 12 times) before giving up with where and why. The start and end levels are forced (the rail cell a person stands in); an end given as coordinates is moved to the ground there when it is within 3 blocks of it (`snap`).
- **Layouts.** `surface` follows the ground (cutting a small rise, decking a dip). `underground` is a tunnel that **runs `depth` blocks below the surface in the middle and comes up to it at both ends** (default 6; the ceiling per column is the ground level minus `min(depth, index, distance to the end)`, and the level closest to it is preferred), and **its horizontal search ignores how the surface rises**, since a tunnel goes through a hill rather than round it (a first version reused the surface costs and routed round every hill). It is lit: a torch every 6 rails wherever there is cover, a courtesy that never fails the plan. `auto` (default) takes the surface unless it needs more than 1.5 cuts and decks per rail, then a tunnel if one can be planned; **it decides for the whole line, not per segment**. Only loaded terrain is planned (about what the bot sees), up to 400 rails; a longer line is built in stages (start the next at the end of the last).
- **Power, as measured on a real server** (an experiment before any code, kept in the notes here because the design rests on it): a **redstone torch on a free cell beside a powered rail, level with it, powers it**; a **stationary cart on a powered rail with a solid block behind it starts by itself**; but a cart on plain rails coasts only about 4 to 10 blocks (from a standing start about 5), so **a lone powered rail every 8 leaves a cart that starts from rest stranded** (found by the first e2e run: it stopped 2 blocks in). Hence: economy powers **the first four rails** (one torch feeds the run, so it gets up to speed on powered rails), then one every `spacing` (7 to 10, default 8) on level track and **every 3 on a slope**, never on a corner (a powered rail cannot bend; corners and their neighbours are level and plain), each run with its own torch. `rich` powers every rail that is not a corner, with a torch on the first of each unbroken run and then one every 9 (power passes up to 8 rails along powered rails). A block (`support_block`, default cobblestone) is set **behind the first rail** unless the way behind is solid, which is what makes a resting cart start.
- **Torch spots** (`torchSpotFor`): a free cell beside the rail with solid ground under it, else a **niche dug into the tunnel wall** (a `break` step of natural material), else a free cell with a support block placed under it. In the rich variant any of the first six rails of a run may carry the torch. When no torch fits, those rails are laid **plain** and the plan says so in `warnings`/`notes`: a powered rail with no power would brake the cart.
- **Engine changes this needed** (all generic, in `PlanExecutor`/`WorkStep`): a `place` step can be `passable` (rails and torches have no collision, so the bot may stand in their cells; without it every pending rail cell was kept free of the bot and it could never enter the tunnel it was digging), `clearedFirst` (wait for the plan's own `break` step to clear the cell instead of breaking it as part of placing, which dug out of order and without the hazard rule), and the railway plan sets `standing: 'inside'` (the executor prefers standing inside the plan's volume; without it, at every step the bot tried to climb out of its own trench to stand beside the line and timed out after 33 s).
- **`BuildRailwaySkill`** (`build_railway`/`plan_railway`, one class with `preview` and `run`, like `ExcavateSkill`): the start is the bot's own cell unless `from` is given; the end is `to` or where `to_player` stands (they must be in view). Materials are rails, powered rails, redstone torches, the support block, and torches for lighting; `fetch_from_storage` brings what is in containers, and the preview and a `missing_materials` failure name **the raw materials to craft what is missing** (`rawMaterialsFor`: whole crafts, 6 iron ingots and a stick make 16 rails, 6 gold ingots, a stick and redstone make 6 powered rails). A cut that meets lava, water, or a cave ends the line (`hazards: 'end_branch'`, one chain for the whole line): the run then fails **`line_incomplete`** saying where and why, and nothing is checked or remembered. The result has `rails`, the layout, `corners` (where it turns), `slopes`, `cuts`, `decks`, `poweredRails`, `torches`, the buffer, the notes, and the verification.
- **Verification and repair** (`RailVerifier`, on by default): after a settling pause every rail is read through `RailAccess` and compared with what its neighbours should give it (`rail-orientation.ts`, pure: straight axis, `ascending_*` towards the higher neighbour, the four curves) and the powered ones with `powered`. A rail out of view is walked up to first (still unread: `unverified`). What is wrong is put right **once**: a missing rail placed again, a wrongly bent or wrong-kind one broken and placed again, a torch that is gone set again; then the line is read once more and what is still wrong is reported (`verification.problems`, with `checked` and `repaired`).
- **`place_minecart`** goes to the start of the named railway, else of the last one built, else the nearest rail within 32 (or the rail `at` given), **stands two blocks to the side of the line** (a free cell with ground under it and no rail in it), and places the cart. **Found by the e2e test: a bot standing on the rails stops the cart** (it ran 2.4 blocks and rested against the bot), so it is never placed from on the line. Riding is up to the player.
- **Not built, planned for this phase (see [the railways phase](../../DEVELOPMENT-PLAN/phases/railways.md)):** choosing surface or tunnel per stretch of one line, landmarks as ends (`set_landmark` is Phase 26; ends are coordinates or a player), and end stops. **In the backlog:** junctions and joining an existing line (an existing rail blocks a route as `existing_rail`), detector and activator rails, stations. **Not planned:** free-form diagonal routes (turns are 90 degrees by construction), power by redstone block (a torch is used), and a route through several dimensions. `place_minecart` is never part of a build: it is a separate request, and a build only sets the powered start and the block behind it that let a cart placed later start by itself.
- **Gotchas found on the way, not specific to railways:** the fake bot treated rails and torches as solid blocks (they have no collision in the game, so no standing spot was ever found in a tunnel); the superflat e2e world has bedrock right under three layers of dirt, and **the pathfinder cannot path at the bottom layer next to bedrock** (a tunnel test at depth 3 there stalled with "no path"), so the tunnel e2e uses a depth that stays above it; and dirt under a rail with open air above it turns to grass by itself, which looks like a stray block in a dump.
- **Tests:** unit for the pure pieces (`rawMaterialsFor`; `headingOf`/`expectedShape`/`isCorner` with every shape; `assignPower` for both variants, slopes, corners, short lines; the A* (turns, obstacles, bounds, limits, cheaper-but-longer); `TerrainReader`; the route planner on a small block world: straight, turns, lava, walls, protected areas, a low rise, a tall rise cut through, a ravine and a lake bridged, trees cut, corners level on a slope, every refusal, tunnels below the surface and a shallow lake, `auto` for both outcomes; the plan builder: rails, torches on each side, niches, decks, no room, the rich fallback, cuts and their `needs`, the buffer, tunnel lighting), `RailVerifier` (every problem and repair, an unseen rail, cancelling), `RailwayLog`, `BuildRailwaySkill` (preview facts and crafting, ends snapped, a player end, refusals, running with fetching, failures and hints, an ended line, not remembered without a world), `PlaceMinecartSkill`, the two adapters on the fake bot (which now works out rail shapes and power like the game), and the three tools. Integration (`test/integration/railway-flow.test.ts`): plan, build with the check, the minecart at the start, a wall gone round with curves, a lit tunnel through a hill, missing materials with what to craft, no route, no destination and no minecart. E2E (`test/e2e/railways/railway.e2e.test.ts`, real Paper, block by block through RCON): a flat economy line (rails, powered rails, torches, buffer, verification, and **a cart placed at the start that runs the whole line**), a line round a wall with curves, a climb over a rise on slopes, the rich variant (every rail powered), a lit tunnel dug through stone, and `plan_railway` changing nothing plus the missing-materials report.
- **Exit criteria:** "build a railway from here to the base, economy variant, and place a minecart" produces a working line, surface or underground, with the powered rails at the stated spacing, and the cart runs it.
