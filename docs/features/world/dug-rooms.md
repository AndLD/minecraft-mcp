# Dug rooms

**Depends on:** [Excavation](excavation.md), [Construction and building](construction-and-building.md).  
**Used by:** no other feature directly.

- **`RoomProvider`** (`feature/dug-rooms`, shape `room` in `src/skills/excavation/providers/room.ts`) is an ordinary `ExcavationProvider`, used through `excavate`/`preview_excavation` like every other shape; no changes to `ExcavationContext` or `ExcavationProvider.generate()` were needed. What is new: reading the wall a player looks at, and a door that is a courtesy rather than a hard requirement.
- **`LookTarget` gained the hit face** (`src/core/container.port.ts`: `blockLookedAt` now also returns `face`, one of `top`/`bottom`/`north`/`south`/`east`/`west`; `MineflayerLookTarget` maps Mineflayer's `BlockFace` enum from `bot.world.raycast`'s result, which itself gained a `face: number` field in `BotLike`).
- **`WallTarget`** (`src/skills/excavation/wall-target.ts`) turns "the wall I'm looking at" into an origin and a direction: it reads `LookTarget.blockLookedAt(player)`, refuses a look at the floor or ceiling (`not_a_wall`) or no look at all (`no_target`), and otherwise digs **opposite the face that was hit** (a north face means the wall faces north, so the room digs south). The origin is the **hit block's own column**, at the **walkable floor found by scanning down the column just outside the wall** (the player's side), falling back to the player's own standing level when no floor is found in range. `at`/`direction` (either or both) skip reading the look when the request already names them.
- **Resolved entirely at the tool layer, not the skill.** `ExcavateTool`/`PreviewExcavationTool` (`src/mcp/tools/excavation-tools.ts`) hold a `WallResolver` (the interface `WallTarget` implements; role-based, so a test can fake just `resolve()` without the concrete class's private fields getting in the way) and, only for `shape: "room"`, call it before ever building the `ExcavateInput`: `at`/`direction`/`for_player` are read out of the raw `params` bag (room's own optional fields, ignored by `generate()` — the tool always fills `direction` in before the shape's zod schema ever sees the params), the resolved origin replaces the generic `origin` field, and the **resolved values, not the original look-based request, are what a resumed task replays** (so resuming does not depend on the player still looking at the same spot). `ExcavateSkill` itself stays completely shape-agnostic.
- **The room's own coordinate frame:** `i` runs from the wall face into the rock (the dig direction), `a` across the width (0 = left as seen entering, following `HEADINGS[direction].right`), `b` is height above the floor. The room is always **horizontally centred on the looked-at column** (`centerIndex = floor((width-1)/2)` maps to world offset 0), whatever `entrance_at` does to the entrance; the entrance's own band (`entrance_width` cells, positioned by `entrance_at`: `center` centres it in the width, `left`/`right` flush it to that edge) is a sub-range of that width, not a shift of the room itself. Every break step's `needs` is computed by a single rule (`before`, no special-casing entrance vs. room): the cell behind it along `i` if that is part of the footprint, else the cell below (`b - 1`), else the neighbour one step towards the "dig root" column (the centre column clamped into the entrance band, so a `left`/`right` entrance still has a real first cell to start from) — this covers the entrance and the room, whatever `entrance_width` and `entrance_at` are, without digging anything from a distance.
- **Entrance modes.** `dig` (default) generates the entrance's own `break` steps first. `player` generates none of them, but the room's front row still `needs` those exact world positions, so nothing is dug until the player has actually opened them; **before the plan runs**, `ExcavateSkill.run()` calls the new, optional `ExcavationProvider.needsConfirmation(params)` hook (returns a `DecisionPrompt` or undefined; only `RoomProvider` uses it) and awaits `context.ask()` — a single option, `done` (cancelling the wait, or answering anything only `resume_task` would send, i.e. `undefined`, aborts without digging anything; with no `context.ask` at all, for example a direct call in a test, it just carries on). `none` digs nothing in front: the room starts at `i = 0`, assuming a hole is already there.
- **The door is a `place` step that is `optional: true`**, a new flag mirroring `break`'s existing `optional` (used for ore veins) but on `place`: counted in `preview`'s `needs` (so it shows up as something the job would use) but **never in `missing`, so it never blocks the plan from starting**, and a shortfall is not a failure of the run — `PlanExecutor` tracks it separately (`PlanPreview.optionalMissing`, fetched from storage by `ExcavateSkill.run()` alongside the ordinary `missing`, exactly like a required item, only non-blocking; `PlanOutcome.missingOptional`, what is still missing after the attempt) and reports it instead of refusing. When the item is not carried, `PlanExecutor.place()` tries `SupplyCrafter.craft(block, 1, ctx)` (the same crafter that already makes spare torches and tools) before giving up on that attempt; a genuinely unreachable door (no materials, no crafting table reachable) just leaves the plan finished with the entrance open and no door, `missingOptional` naming it. One door per entrance cell (so a 2-wide entrance gets two), placed in the entrance's outermost row (`i = 0`, the wall face), turned to lie in the wall's plane with `alignAxis` (the axis of `HEADINGS[direction].forward` — `x` for east/west, `z` for north/south — exactly the house blueprint's own door convention). `door: true` resolves to a plain `oak_door`; an `entrance_height` above 2 leaves a gap above a 2-high door, reported in the plan's new, generic `WorkPlan.warnings` (an optional string array `PlanExecutor.preview()` passes straight through into `PlanPreview.warnings` — inert plumbing any future provider can also use).
- **Gotcha found while building it, not room-specific:** a `break` step and a `place` step can legitimately target the **same cell** (the entrance floor a door goes into, once dug) — nothing else in the codebase had done this before, and it surfaced a real bug in the shared executor: its own verification pass re-derives "already open" from `blockAt()` alone, and a door (or any block a `place` step puts there) is not `air`/`ladder`/`torch`, so the **second round dug the just-placed door back out**, leaving `used` and `missingOptional` both listing it (placed once, then destroyed). Fixed generically: `PlanExecutor` now also treats a cell as "open" for a `break` step when it holds a block **this same plan's own `place` steps intend to put there** (`placedBlocksOf(plan)`, consulted by `isOpening()` and by `preview()`'s own `done`/`toBreak` split) — not specific to doors, so any future shape that places into a cell it also digs gets this for free.
- **Gotcha, also generic:** two torches on **opposite** side walls of a narrow room are `width - 1` blocks apart, which can be closer than `torch_spacing` (the along-the-wall cadence) — `SiteSafety.needsTorch`'s own "no torch closer than `spacing`" check does not know the two are on different walls, and silently skipped the second one (found first at width 3, then again at width 2, where it takes a `spacing` of exactly 1 — a capped constant is not enough). Room's light steps use `spacing: Math.min(torch_spacing, width - 1)`, a value for "how close counts as redundant" kept separate from "how far apart along the depth", not reused from `torch_spacing` itself.
- **Gotcha found by the e2e test, also generic, not room-specific:** with `entrance: player`, the room's own first cells `need` a real-world position the plan never digs itself (the player's own cut). `PlanExecutor` used to give up on a whole chain **the instant** every remaining step's `needs` read as still blocked, with no allowance for a block that was in fact just opened (by RCON in the test; by the player's own pickaxe a moment before saying "done" in real play) but has not yet reached the bot's own world view — a single stale read could silently skip the entire room while the run still reported `succeeded`. `PlanExecutor.settling()` now polls a few times (`SETTLE_POLLS` × `SETTLE_POLL_MS`) before actually giving up on such a chain; harmless for a chain that really is permanently blocked (a hazard-ended tunnel), since it still gives up the same way, just a moment later.
- **Tests:** unit for `WallTarget` (each horizontal face's dig direction, floor/ceiling refusal, the floor scan and its player-position fallback, explicit `at`/`direction` overriding the look, either alone or together), `RoomProvider` (entrance and room cell counts and positions, the single true root and every `needs` pointing into the plan, the probe list excluding the way in, `entrance_at` `left`/`right` against each edge, `entrance_width` 1–3 and the matching door count, `entrance: none`/`player`, `needsConfirmation` only for `player`, torches along the side walls and their count, `door: true`, the height-gap warning, `entrance_width`/`entrance_height` bigger than the room refused by zod, no resolved direction refused by `generate`), `ExcavateSkill`'s asking (waits, proceeds on `done`, aborts on a cancelled wait, carries on with no `context.ask`, never asks for `entrance: dig` or any other shape), and `PlanExecutor`'s new optional-placement behaviour (finishes and reports what's missing instead of failing, counted in `needs` but never `missing`, the supplies-crafter fallback); the two tool classes' wall resolution (`excavation-tools.test.ts`); integration through the MCP server on a stateful fake world with a fake look target (entrance, room, door, and torches from a look at a wall; preview without digging; `not_a_wall`); e2e (`test/e2e/excavation/room.e2e.test.ts`) digging into a real stone hill from the player's own look with a door and torches, `entrance: player` waiting then finishing once the player cuts it by hand and answers `resume_task`, and water behind the wall stopping a column one block short.
